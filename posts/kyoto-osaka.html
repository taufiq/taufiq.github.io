Google Reviews

On my 7-day trip with Suah, I realised that we had ~17 meals to eat in Japan, and we wanted to have the best possible food experience. However, using Google Reviews' aggregated ratings were a hit or miss. Using Tabelog (Japan's own curated food review app) helped – but not by much. I guess there are some finer details that we were missing out. So here is my mildly controversial take on how to better gauge reviews through trial-and-error during our Japan trip, ranked in order:
  1. Ethnic Approval

    2. Seems simple enough, but I do think if you're trying ethnic cuisine, we should be adding heavy weight to reviews of those with same ethnicity saying things like "this reminds me of home"., or the opposite. It's not 100% accurate but it helped me gauge it better!

  2. Descriptive Long Texts

    3. As someone with a friend who likes lattes with syrup in it (im a purist), he's made me come to terms that people's tastes have no bounds. Your favourite resturant might not be mine, and that's fine! I think these descriptive reviews contain nuances in the taste profile of the dishes that can help you make a better decision by cross-checking against your own preferences.

  3. Multiple Sources

    4. Tabelog was a lifesaver... Besides their interesting rating that makes you think all resturants are average 3 stars, the extra source helps us to scope to see what japanese people loved and find places that they liked.

  4. Outlets matter!

    5. Suah loved the first outlet of Onigiri Gori-chan so much that we went to try it again at a similarly rated outlet but we were left quite disappointed.

  5. Forgiveness

    6. This is similar to the previous point but I think if it sucked the first time, give it another go (ok if it's really bad then don't). E.g If we swapped our onigiri outlet order, we would've probably marked it off as a terrible franchise and never discovered the better one. But this applies to single outlet ones as well.
